YOUR RECOMMENDATIONS

FROM WOODCRAFT TO FDM

Printability toolkit

01 / CLEARANCE

Fit is not the same as wood

PLA cannot compress like wood fibers. A useful exploratory FDM sliding-fit range is 0.20–0.30 mm clearance per side. Tune it with calibration parts, not guesses.

02 / ORIENTATION

Mind the layers

Orient the connection to avoid pulling printed layers apart. Favor broad load-bearing faces and put high tensile stresses in the layer plane where practical.

03 / RETENTION

Lock the assembly path

A dovetail blocks lifting but still slides out along the insertion direction. Add a stop, a printed transverse key, or a retaining wedge where needed.

04 / FILAMENT

Use the right material

PLA gives crisp profiles but is relatively brittle; PETG is more forgiving for some reusable clips; TPU works for flexible retaining parts.

05 / GEOMETRY

Soften the corners

Use generous fillets at stressed roots, 0.5–1 mm entry chamfers, and avoid thin necks and support-filled internal sockets.

06 / PROTOTYPING

Test tiny coupons first

Print 15–25 mm trial sections with several clearances, then assess tightness and wear through repeated assembly cycles before printing the full part.

Printed snap fits can fatigue or crack, especially across layer lines. Do not use untested 3D-printed joints for overhead, human-supporting, or other safety-critical structures.

↔ Withdrawal

Does a joint resist being pulled apart? A sliding dovetail resists transverse separation but may move along its assembly axis.

⇋ Shear

Can components slide sideways? Mortises and captured grooves distribute shear through contact faces.

⤴ Racking

Could a rectangular frame deform sideways? Braces, shoulders, and multi-face corners improve stability.

◇ Locking mechanism

Friction, gravity, geometric interlock, captured assembly, keyed stops, and wedges are not equivalent.

⟲ Reversibility

Choose removable keys and wedges for repairable modules. Hidden expansion wedges often create permanent assemblies.

▧ FDM anisotropy

Printed parts can be weakest between layers. Designing a load path is as important as choosing the joint name.
